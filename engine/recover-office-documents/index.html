A DOCX is a ZIP — and that is good news

Word, Excel and PowerPoint files (.docx, .xlsx, .pptx) are ZIP packages in the Open Packaging Conventions format: XML parts for the text, sheets and slides, media files, a [Content_Types].xml list and a web of relationship files tying them together. Each part is stored as a ZIP entry with its own CRC-32.

So when Office says a document is corrupt, the question is which parts still pass their checksum. The engine’s Office / OOXML semantic recovery answers it: it recovers every part whose CRC-32 matches, checks the content-type list and the relationship graph, and reports exactly which parts are missing or damaged.

What it recovers — and what it will not invent

What the engine proves

  • Every part whose bytes still match their CRC-32 — document XML, worksheets, slides, images and other media.
  • The content-type list and the relationship graph, checked for parts that are referenced but missing.
  • A report that names which parts are verified, damaged or absent.
  • The ZIP container itself, rebuilt by structural repair if its index is broken.

What it will not claim

  • It does not invent missing application content just to make the document open.
  • It does not rewrite damaged XML into something plausible.
  • It does not open password-protected (encrypted) Office files.
  • It does not guarantee that Word, Excel or PowerPoint will open the result.

Recover a corrupted Word, Excel or PowerPoint file

  1. Open the full engine and add the .docx, .xlsx or .pptx file.
  2. Run Analyze a file to see whether the container or individual parts are damaged.
  3. If the ZIP index is broken, run Structural repair first.
  4. Open Recover contents, choose Office document (DOCX, XLSX, PPTX) and press Run.
  5. Download the verified parts and the report. In a Word file the text lives in word/document.xml; in Excel the sheets are under xl/worksheets/, in PowerPoint the slides under ppt/slides/, and images sit in each package’s media folder.

Also works on other ZIP packages

The same method applies to other ZIP-based packages, because the evidence is the same: a per-entry CRC-32. To look inside a document without extracting anything, use Look inside; to pull out individual entries safely, use safe extraction.

Questions

Can I recover a corrupted Word document without uploading it?

Yes. The engine runs in your browser and reads the .docx from your disk. It recovers every part whose CRC-32 still matches and gives you the parts as downloads.

Will Word open the recovered file?

Not necessarily, and the engine does not promise it. It returns what can be proven intact; it does not fill in missing parts to force the document to open. The text itself is in word/document.xml.

What about Excel “we found a problem with some content”?

Run Analyze first: it shows whether the container or specific parts are damaged. Recovery then returns the worksheets whose bytes verify.

Can it open a password-protected Office file?

No. Encrypted documents are detected and reported, not decrypted.
