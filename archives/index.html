Archive recovery

An archive is a container with a table of contents, and almost every recovery question about one reduces to a single thing: which of its two descriptions of itself survived. This section collects what we have measured about reading damaged archives — including the case where the same measurement told us a solver was not worth writing.

Why a ZIP is recoverable at all

A ZIP describes its contents twice. Each entry carries a local file header immediately before its data, and the archive ends with a central directory listing every entry again — name, sizes, checksum and where its data begins. The two are written at different times to different places.

That redundancy is the whole basis of ZIP recovery, and it decides what each kind of damage costs:

what is destroyedwhat remainswhat it costs
the central directoryevery local headerthe index — entries must be found by scanning, and nothing independently attests which of them belonged to the archive
one local headerthe directory record for itthe payload can still be located, because the directory says where it starts and how long it is
both, for one entry—that entry, unless its bytes can be bounded by its neighbours
the end recordthe directory itself, usually intact just before itthe anchor — and a reader may find some other archive’s end record instead

The last row is the subject of the first report above. The others are why an archive with a destroyed directory is usually recoverable, and why an archive whose data is destroyed is usually not.

A second copy is evidence; a self-consistent record is not. A local header carries its own checksum, so a header and its payload can agree with each other while nothing attests that either belonged to this archive. The directory record is what turns a found entry into an attested one.

The rule this section runs on

  • Nothing is emitted that a checksum did not verify, and an entry found only by scanning is labelled as exactly that rather than mixed in with attested ones.
  • Surface before solver. Each measurement counts what is addressable before anything is built, and two of the reports here exist because that count said stop.
  • Candidate rules are falsified against the whole corpus before code is written — a fix that recovers eight cases and mis-fires on three hundred is not a fix.
  • Negative results are published. A method measured and abandoned is a result, and it is the cheapest one to hide.
