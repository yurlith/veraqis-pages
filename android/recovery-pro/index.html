Who it is for

Anyone holding a ZIP that will not open, will not fully extract, or fails on one or more entries, and needs to know which files can be proven intact before deciding what to do next.

What damage it handles

The stable ZIP recovery scope for the first commercial release, grounded in the same recovery engine already benchmarked on these damage classes:

  • Missing end-of-central-directory record
  • Damaged central directory
  • Stale central-directory offsets
  • Local-header scanning when the index cannot be trusted
  • Truncated-entry detection
  • CRC-32 verification of every recovered entry
  • False-signature rejection
  • Overlap detection between entries
  • Recovery of intact entries before and after a damaged region
  • Stored and deflated entries
  • ZIP64, where the full path is verified

This scope is Android's closed-test target, grounded in the engine's own benchmark record (see CLAUDE.md "Current technical baseline" — findings F-1, F-2B, F-3 and F-4 closed on the controlled corpus, zero false recoveries). The Android cross-product integration for this scope is not yet publicly shipped.

How analysis works

A working ZIP reader starts at the end, reads the central directory, and trusts it — fast for a healthy file, useless for a damaged one, because the index is what's most likely missing. Recovery Pro reads the other way round: every entry carries its own local file header next to its data, so scanning for those headers reconstructs the archive's contents without depending on the index at all. Where the central directory is damaged, it is rebuilt from what the local headers prove, never invented.

VERIFIED / DAMAGED / UNKNOWN / LOST

A result is only called verified when something independent of the repair confirms it. A structural fix does not get to certify its own output.

LabelWhat it meansWhat proved it
VerifiedThese are the original bytes.The entry decompressed cleanly and its stored CRC-32 matched.
DamagedThe entry exists but its contents changed.The entry decompressed, but the CRC-32 did not match.
UnknownWe cannot say either way.No surviving evidence settles it.
LostThe data is not in the file.The bytes are absent — truncated away or overwritten.

A report that says "43 verified, 2 damaged, 1 unknown" is more useful than one that says "repaired!" and leaves you to discover which files are quietly wrong.

Relationship to the free preview

The free VERAQIS Archiver includes a limited recovery preview — damage class, discovered entries, an estimated recoverable size. Recovery Pro is the next step: it recovers the entries the preview identified, verifies each one, and writes them to a destination you choose with a full report. Free tells you whether it's worth pursuing; Pro does the recovery.

Read-only source, separate destination

Read-only source guarantee

The source archive is opened read-only and never modified. A failed recovery attempt costs you nothing — the original stays exactly as it was.

Recovery destination

Recovered output is written to a destination you choose, separate from the source. Nothing is repaired in place.

Report format

Every recovery produces a manifest, not a claim: entry names, verdicts, byte ranges, rule IDs, and before/after hashes, so the result can be checked later rather than taken on trust. See a published example on the test record and the reports page.

Honest limitations

  • No password recovery. Encrypted archives are detected and reported, not opened.
  • No encryption bypass. Recovery Pro does not remove or circumvent access controls.
  • No reconstruction of bytes that are absent. If the data was truncated away or overwritten, no analysis returns it — it is reported as lost.
  • No guarantee every damaged ZIP is recoverable. Recovery depends entirely on what survived.
  • No in-place modification of the source. The original file is never rewritten.
  • No malware verdict. This is a structural recovery tool, not an antivirus.
  • No legal or forensic certification. Recovery output is not presented as court-admissible or forensically certified evidence.
  • Other formats are not part of this commercial promise. PDF, SQLite, 7z, RAR, TAR, GZIP, XZ, BZIP2, Office and other formats have experimental engine-level work in the repository, but none of it is part of Recovery Pro's stable scope. See the format reference for the honest per-format position.

Recovery Pro questions

Is VERAQIS Recovery Pro available now?

No. Recovery Pro is in closed Android testing. There is no public APK and no Google Play listing yet.

Is Recovery Pro the same app as VERAQIS Archiver with more features unlocked?

No. Recovery Pro is a separate, specialised app for one workflow: recovering a damaged ZIP. It is not VERAQIS Archiver Premium or an ad-free version of the free app.

Can it recover any damaged ZIP?

No. Recovery depends on what survived. Data that is gone stays gone; Recovery Pro proves what the surviving bytes support and reports the rest as damaged, unknown or lost.

Can it open a password-protected ZIP without the password?

No. Encrypted archives are detected and reported. Recovery Pro does not guess, derive or brute-force a password, and does not bypass encryption.
